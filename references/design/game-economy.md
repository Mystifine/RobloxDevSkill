# Game economy, monetization and live ops

Companion to [game-design.md](game-design.md): load it when a design or a system touches currencies, pacing, odds, prices, offers or the update cadence. Its results go in the plan's Economy, Monetization and Live calendar sections ([assets/templates/GAME_DESIGN_PLAN.md](../../assets/templates/GAME_DESIGN_PLAN.md)).

## Economy and pacing

- **Few currencies:** one soft (earned by the core loop, spent on power), one premium or rare (earned slowly, may be bought), and a material only when it enables a distinct decision (shards that evolve units). At most three on the HUD; each has sources, sinks, a place on screen and a reason.
- **Faucets and sinks**, tabled per phase with rates per minute. Sinks scale with income, or currency piles up; rich players get fantasy sinks (cosmetics, rerolls, fusions).
- **Offline earnings:** a share of the online rate (10-50%) up to a cap (2-12 h), paid with a welcome-back moment; online stays clearly better.
- **Trading makes the economy a market:** control supply (drop rates, limited items, untradeable starters), add item sinks (fusion consumes copies), keep trades atomic and server-side with a log, and block trading paid items where `IsPaidItemTradingAllowed` is false.
- **Chance arithmetic** for a 1-in-N tier at luck L: chance per roll L/N; half of players have it after about 0.69 N/L rolls, 90% after about 2.3 N/L; after N/L rolls 37% still have none, after 3 N/L 5%. Set target times on the median and the 90th percentile, not the mean. Pity at 2-3x the expected rolls rescues only the unluckiest 5-14% and removes the worst stories. Cap stacked luck (luck × server luck × weather × passes multiplies fast) so the top tier stays rare. The odds shown come from the same function and data the server rolls with. A guaranteed item per banner multiplies across banners, so size pity at the pool you will ship: a pity of 50 suited a six-item pool, and at the launch pool finished the collection in 4.5 h against a 25 h target. A paid pull is worth buying only when free pulls are scarce against the chase: at a free faucet of 11-19 pulls a day every free player got every headliner within its window, so paid pulls changed nothing. Measure the faucet from real session and match lengths (real matches ran 135 s against a modeled 75 s, halving free pulls), then simulate free and paying players per banner window before setting pity and prices.
- **Simulate, don't guess.** Copy [scripts/PacingSim.luau](../../scripts/PacingSim.luau) to `tools/pacing/` beside a `model.luau` *(default)* and run it with Lune. Require the game's constants modules where they are pure data, so the plan and the game share one source of truth. Model a prestige as a `save` buy whose `buy` resets the run and adds the bonus, chance as `math.random` in `step`, real-time timers in `away`, and saving for a zone as a `save` buy that becomes available once it is a few minutes of income away. Run `simulate` with the cheapest and the value player (`simulateMany` when there is chance) and paste `report`, `curve` and `rngTable` into the plan. Where buys add progress and no income (packs in a collection game), `value` has nothing to rank: give each buy a `worth` (its expected new cards or power) and run the `worth` player instead. Re-run whenever the numbers change.
- **No Lune:** the blueprint's `rokit.toml` does not pin it, and adding it makes Rokit ask the owner to trust `lune-org/lune`, an approval only they give. Meanwhile `rojo build` a copy, open it in its own Studio window, run the model with `python scripts/studio_mcp.py luau model.luau --studio <that copy>` and a first line `--@use PacingSim`, and close only that window after. Write `local PacingSim = PacingSim or require("./PacingSim")` so the model runs both ways.

## Monetization

Products: game passes (permanent), developer products (repeatable), subscriptions, private servers, paid access, and rewarded video ads where the experience and player are eligible (`AdService`). Creator Rewards pay for engagement itself, so retention is revenue too.

- **Sell amplifiers of the fantasy:** luck, speed, extra slots, more income, cosmetics, VIP, server-wide boosts that make the buyer a hero. Never sell relief from friction added to be sold.
- **The free path finishes the game.** Every power item is reachable free, only slower; in PvP, paid power is capped and cosmetics carry the revenue.
- **Offer at the moment of desire, never in the way:** a full hotbar → more slots; a friend's aura → that cosmetic. No prompts during the core action or the tutorial, and a cap on unsolicited offers per session.
- **Price ladder:** a cheap starter pack of obvious value (the first purchase), impulse items, core passes, high-end items. Passes that stack multiply, so cap the stack.
- **Paid random items** ([policy](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items)): anything random bought with Robux *or with currency Robux can buy* (rolls, eggs, spins, capsules, rerolls, random enhancements, fusions), plus the luck boosts and pity that change its odds. Before the spend, show every outcome's odds as percentages summing to 100% (a list behind a labeled button, not a bare (i)); explain boosts with numbers and keep the shown odds live while one is active; update odds when outcomes run out. When `PolicyService:GetPolicyInfoForPlayerAsync` reports `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted`, give that player one of the policy's alternatives (a free earnable path, a fixed disclosed order, direct purchase) or hide or block the item. A game whose roll currency can be bought falls under this policy. Before selling one, check that its worst outcome has a use, and stop offering it once it can only give duplicates *(default)*.
- **Reuse a developer product or pass in place:** `PATCH https://apis.roblox.com/developer-products/v2/universes/{id}/developer-products/{productId}` and `.../game-passes/v1/universes/{id}/game-passes/{passId}`, multipart with name, description and imageFile, return 204 and keep the ids. Snapshot them first: a rename shows at once in any game still on that universe. `GetProductInfo` gives the viewer's regional price, so hardcoded prices drift.
- **Resolve an id the user calls a place id** before acting on it: `universes/v1/places/{id}/universe`, then `games/v1/games?universeIds={id}`, and report the game's name and owner. Universe ids now reach the size place ids once had: one such id returned no universe as a place and was another creator's script as an asset.
- **Receipts:** one `ProcessReceipt`, idempotent, granted only after the save succeeds ([coding.md](../code/coding.md)).
- **Track the economy:** `AnalyticsService:LogEconomyEvent(player, flowType, currencyType, amount, endingBalance, transactionType, itemSku, customFields)` for every source and sink, on the server after the transaction succeeds, never on the attempt. `amount` is always positive (the dashboard shows sinks as negative); `endingBalance` is the balance after it. Start from `Enum.AnalyticsEconomyTransactionType` (`IAP`, `TimedReward`, `Onboarding`, `Shop`, `Gameplay`, `ContextualPurchase`, sent as `.Name`) or name your own, and keep types and SKUs stable: past 20 transaction types and 100 SKUs the rest group as "Other". Plan for five currencies: the economy page shows five, the limits table allows 10 resource types. Sources minus sinks should stay near zero; a growing net or a rising average wallet, payers' above all, calls for sinks. A checkout funnel measures the steps to a purchase; the economy event is its ledger ([docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/economy-events)).

## Live ops and content

- Plan the cadence from day one: an update every 1-2 weeks with an in-game countdown, an event each month, seasonal events, team-hosted live events (server-wide weather, bosses or luck the team triggers).
- **Content is data:** a hero, zone, item or event is a data row plus assets, not new code, so an update is mostly content. Estimate the cost of one content unit (model, data, effects) and fit the roadmap to the team. Each new unit gets its own new assets *(default)*: reusing existing clips or effects to make new content cheaper is the user's call, so offer it as an option, never plan it in.
- **Roadmap along the direction:** 6-12 weeks, each update a headline, its content and the one twist it adds.
- Limited-time power never disappears for good: events come back, and event currency rules are clear.
- **A countdown ends when the item really leaves:** scan the rotation forward until it is no longer offered, since a rerun can bring a banner straight back and a naive end-of-week countdown would end while it stays.
