# Monetization

Products, prices, the shop, offers, gifts and purchase announcements. Currencies, pacing, odds maths and live ops are in [game-economy.md](game-economy.md). Results go in the plan's Monetization section ([assets/templates/GAME_DESIGN_PLAN.md](../../assets/templates/GAME_DESIGN_PLAN.md)).

Products: passes (permanent), developer products (repeatable), subscriptions, private servers, paid access, and rewarded video ads where the experience and player are eligible (`AdService`). Creator Rewards pay for engagement itself, so retention is revenue too.

**Every game ships these** unless its plan says why not *(default)*: purchase announcements, lucky purchases, a value ladder up to a whale tier, urgent offers, gifts, regional pricing on cosmetics only, product and banner art, contextual offers paced by one popup queue, products placed where players look, value-based discounts and tags, and paid random items and trading inside PolicyService.

## Products and prices

- **Sell amplifiers of the fantasy:** luck, speed, extra slots, more income, cosmetics, VIP, server-wide boosts that make the buyer a hero. Never sell relief from friction added to be sold.
- **The free path finishes the game.** Every power item is reachable free, only slower; in PvP, paid power is capped and cosmetics carry the revenue.
- **Price ladder:** a cheap starter pack of obvious value (the first purchase), impulse items, core passes, high-end items and a whale tier. Each step up gives more per Robux than the one below, its bonus shown against the base pack ("+25%") *(default)*. The whale tier is several times the next price, with the best rate and status others see (an exclusive aura or title, a gift to the whole server, every pass in one bundle); in PvP it is status inside the power cap. Passes that stack multiply, so cap the stack.
- **Regional pricing by product** *(default)*: on for what only changes looks (cosmetics, effects, visual upgrades); off for anything that adds currency, power, luck or progress, or can be traded: a regional price can be 30% of the default, and value bought cheaply flows into trades, leaderboards and fights. Managed Pricing (regional prices plus price tests) enrolls new games and new items automatically and prices passes regionally by default, so set `isRegionalPricingEnabled` on every product and pass (the `PATCH` below) and read `priceInformation.enabledFeatures` back: an economy item lists no `RegionalPricing`. A developer product needs runtime prices and transfer rules before it is enabled: `MarketplaceService:GetUsersPriceLevelsAsync(userIds)` returns `PriceLevelInfo` entries (`UserId`, `PriceLevel`), the level running 1 to 1000 (full price) as the share of the global price that player pays, read at join and kept for the session only; Roblox's samples allow a gift when the sender's level is at least the recipient's, and a trade between equal levels. A player's economic location comes from signals such as VPN use, billing and account history (it can differ from the account's country); when it cannot be told, the default price shows, and regional prices update periodically. Subscriptions priced in Robux, private servers and developer servers are regionalized automatically. Opting a game in asks for typed prices to be fixed first; price tests read demand, conversion, spend and spenders by country, and leave items under their eligibility threshold alone.
- **Prices are read, never typed:** `GetProductInfoAsync` for a product or pass (`GetProductInfo` is deprecated), `GetDeveloperProductsAsync` for every product. `PriceInRobux` is what the viewing account pays; `UserBasePriceInRobux` is the set price. One tester read every product 20% under its set price in two games (349 as 280), one of them with no regional pricing in `priceInformation.enabledFeatures`: read that and compare the two fields before calling it regional pricing. The Dynamic Price Check (the Passes or Developer Products page's menu) finds typed prices: up to five test accounts, each Price pinned (a fixed fake Robux amount) or Location pinned (a fake economic location); enable it, wait a few minutes, play, and disable it after. Managed Pricing on passes sold only on the game's details page needs no check.
- **Product art:** every product's icon is in the game's icon family ([icons.md](../ui/icons.md)), and the same art is the product's own image (`imageFile`: a .jpg, .png or .bmp of at most 512x512, the subject inside the inscribed circle) that Roblox shows on its own surfaces, purchase prompts and the store; none ships with a default or placeholder image.
- **Reuse a developer product or pass before creating one** *(default)*, in place: `PATCH https://apis.roblox.com/developer-products/v2/universes/{id}/developer-products/{productId}` and `.../game-passes/v1/universes/{id}/game-passes/{passId}`, multipart with name, description and imageFile, return 204 and keep the ids. They need the project's key with `developer-product:write` and `game-pass:write`: the Studio MCP's `open_cloud_invoke` listed both but could not PATCH them. Snapshot them first and diff the change against it (a dry run): a rename shows at once in any game still on that universe, so dashboard changes ship with the publish that grants them. Keep every product the game sells on sale *(default)*.
- **Resolve an id the user calls a place id** before acting on it: `universes/v1/places/{id}/universe`, then `games/v1/games?universeIds={id}`, and report the game's name and owner. Universe ids now reach the size place ids once had: one such id returned no universe as a place and was another creator's script as an asset.

## Shop and offers

- **Placement** *(default)*: the best products stand where every player walks (spawn, the lobby, the way to the core loop) as displays that open their purchase: the pet on a plinth, the aura on a mannequin. The shop opens on its featured offers, the best products on the first screen.
- **Banner art** *(default)*: featured offers and events get wide hero art in the game's style, made the way [icons.md](../ui/icons.md) makes images, with names and prices as native text.
- **Tags and discounts** *(default)*: BEST VALUE on the best rate, MOST POPULAR from sales, NEW for a set time after release. A product genuinely worth more than its price shows its discount against its original value: its contents at their own prices, or its currency at the base pack's rate ("-40%", "worth 2,500"); never a former price it never sold at, which is deceptive pricing.
- **Urgency is real:** limited stock is counted across servers (a MemoryStore `UpdateAsync`), and a countdown ends when it says, saved, never restarted on rejoin. Reserve a unit before prompting and release it when the server's `PromptProductPurchaseFinished` reports no purchase or after a timeout; grant a receipt even if stock ran out meanwhile.
- **Offer at the moment of desire, never in the way:** a full hotbar → more slots; a friend's aura → that cosmetic; a death → a revive; out of coins → a pack; exclusive deals at rejoins, milestones and events where they help. No prompts during the core action or the tutorial.
- **One popup queue** *(default)*: every window the player did not open (daily reward, offers, update notes) goes through one queue: one at a time, by priority, a cap per session and a gap between, its counts and each offer's last showing saved in the profile so a quick rejoin does not reset them. A rejoin with nothing else queued shows the exclusive offer at once; after onboarding or a daily reward, the offer waits about 5 minutes or for the next common goal (a stage cleared, back at base). A declined offer cools down; closing is one tap.

## Purchases

- **Receipts:** one `ProcessReceipt`, idempotent on `PurchaseId`, returning `PurchaseGranted` only after the save succeeds ([coding.md](../code/coding.md)); a grant that adds currency is logged as an economy event ([game-economy.md](game-economy.md#economy-and-pacing)). Passes are granted on the server's `PromptGamePassPurchaseFinished`; nothing is granted from `PromptProductPurchaseFinished`. A receipt carries only `PurchaseId`, `PlayerId`, `ProductId`, `PlaceIdWherePurchased`, `CurrencySpent`, `CurrencyType` and `ProductPurchaseChannel`; an unresolved one runs again only when the player buys again or rejoins (the player must be in the server for it to run, and a yielding callback has no timeout), and is never refunded. A `PurchaseGranted` return can still fail to be recorded, leaving the purchase to run again, which idempotency absorbs.
- **Save what the moment decides before prompting:** a pot to double, a revive on the spot, a gift's recipient. Grant from the saved value (a reward kind paying the held amount times the multiplier kept the receipt idempotent and fair), block the free path to the same claim while the prompt is open, and keep such products unlisted (`storePageEnabled` false): a Listed product also sells outside the game, its receipt arriving when the buyer next joins, where no moment exists. A receipt that arrives after its moment still pays: a stored revive, the held pot, a gift credit the buyer sends later.
- **Gifts** *(default)*: everything sold can be bought for a player in the server. A pass is gifted through its own developer product, which sets an owned flag in the recipient's profile; the pass check reads the flag or `UserOwnsGamePassAsync`. Before prompting, check that the recipient does not already own a one-time item, that a paid random item suits both players' policies, and that a regionally priced gift passes the price-level rule. A recipient who left gets it through an inbox key (`UpdateAsync`) claimed on join; acknowledge after that write. Gifts roll luck and are announced like purchases.
- **Announcements** *(default)*: every purchase in the buyer's server, and big ones (top tiers, above a set price) in every server. After each grant the server sends ids (buyer, product, recipient) to its clients, which write the line with `TextChatService.TextChannels.RBXSystem:DisplaySystemMessage` (its second argument, `metadata`, tags the message type): it runs only on the client, shows only to that player and is neither filtered nor localized, so it carries names and the game's own strings, and text a player typed (a gift note) is filtered on the server first (`TextService:FilterStringAsync`, `GetNonChatStringForBroadcastAsync`). Big purchases also go out on one `MessagingService` topic, with `game.JobId` so the sender skips its own copy. Delivery is best-effort. A message holds 1 kB; a server sends at most 600 + 240 × its players messages a minute and holds 20 + 8 × its players subscriptions (240 subscribe requests a minute); the experience receives (400 + 200 × servers) a minute, and one topic (40 + 80 × servers), about 80 broadcasts a minute however many servers run: set the bar for big so global traffic stays under it. Roblox says these limits may change. A failed publish never touches the grant.
- **Lucky purchases** *(default)*: each purchase, gifts included, has a shown chance to start a boost for everyone in the buyer's server, announced there with the buyer's name. Keep it minor and short: a second trigger adds time instead of stacking, and the boost counts in the luck cap and the pacing model ([game-economy.md](game-economy.md#economy-and-pacing)). It is a paid random item: show the chance with the products, and a buyer whose `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted` is true gets no roll, or a fixed share of a shown server boost meter.

## Paid random items

The [policy](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items) covers anything random bought with Robux *or with currency Robux can buy* (rolls, eggs, spins, capsules, rerolls, random enhancements, fusions, lucky-purchase rolls), plus the luck boosts and pity that change its odds. Before the spend, show every outcome's odds as percentages summing to 100% (a list behind a labeled button, not a bare (i); rounded values say they are rounded); explain boosts with numbers and keep the shown odds live while one is active; update odds when outcomes run out. When `PolicyService:GetPolicyInfoForPlayerAsync` reports `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted`, give that player one of the policy's alternatives (a free earnable path, a fixed disclosed order, direct purchase at the expected value) or hide the item, block its purchase with a message, or keep the player out of the parts of the game that sell it. Random rewards earned free need no odds. Trading paid items is in [game-economy.md](game-economy.md#economy-and-pacing). A game whose roll currency can be bought falls under this policy. Before selling one, check that its worst outcome has a use, and stop offering it once it can only give duplicates *(default)*.
