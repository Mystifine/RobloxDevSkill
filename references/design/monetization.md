Results go in the plan's Monetization section ([assets/templates/GAME_DESIGN_PLAN.md](../../assets/templates/GAME_DESIGN_PLAN.md)). Products: passes (permanent), developer products (repeatable), subscriptions, private servers, paid access, and rewarded video ads where the experience and player are eligible (`AdService`). Creator Rewards pay for engagement itself, so retention is revenue too.

**Every game ships these** unless its plan says why not *(default)*: purchase announcements, lucky purchases, a value ladder up to a whale tier, urgent offers, gifts at one price everywhere, Managed Pricing only on direct, untradeable cosmetics, product and banner art, contextual offers paced by one popup queue, products placed where players look, value-based discounts and tags, and paid random items and trading inside PolicyService.

## Products and prices

- **Sell amplifiers of the fantasy:** luck, speed, extra slots, more income, cosmetics, VIP, server-wide boosts that make the buyer a hero. Never sell relief from friction added to be sold.
- **The free path finishes the game.** Every power item is reachable free, only slower; in PvP, paid power is capped and cosmetics carry the revenue.
- **Price ladder:** a cheap starter pack of obvious value (the first purchase), impulse items, core passes, high-end items and a whale tier. Each step up gives more per Robux than the one below, its bonus shown against the base pack ("+25%") *(default)*. The whale tier is several times the next price, with the best rate and status others see (an exclusive aura or title, a gift to the whole server, every pass in one bundle); in PvP it is status inside the power cap. Passes that stack multiply, so cap the stack.
- **Managed Pricing only where a cheap price breaks nothing** *(default)*. Managed Pricing (Roblox's regional prices plus price tests) goes on an item only when all three hold: it is bought directly, not as a gift; it cannot be traded; and it has no effect on the economy (cosmetics, effects, visual upgrades). It stays off for anything that adds currency, power, luck or progress, or can be traded: a regional price can be 30% of the default, and value bought cheaply flows into trades, leaderboards and fights. **A game whose economy regional prices could break uses none.** Gifts sell at one price everywhere (Gifts, below): one game ran Managed Pricing on all 82 of its items, the products that gift a pass included, until its owner had those 8 switched off while the direct purchases kept it. Roblox's docs say Managed Pricing enrolls new games and new items automatically and prices passes regionally by default, while items created through Open Cloud before the owner accepted it for the game came up off, so set it on each item and read `priceInformation.enabledFeatures` back: an item kept off lists no `RegionalPricing` (the switch, `isManagedPricingEnabled`, and its refusals: [product-setup.md](product-setup.md)). A developer product needs runtime prices and transfer rules before it is enabled: `MarketplaceService:GetUsersPriceLevelsAsync(userIds)` returns `PriceLevelInfo` entries (`UserId`, `PriceLevel`), the level running 1 to 1000 (full price) as the share of the global price that player pays, read at join and kept for the session only; Roblox's samples allow a gift when the sender's level is at least the recipient's, and a trade between equal levels. A player's economic location comes from signals such as VPN use, billing and account history (it can differ from the account's country); when it cannot be told, the default price shows, and regional prices update periodically. Subscriptions priced in Robux, private servers and developer servers are regionalized automatically. Opting a game in asks for typed prices to be fixed first; price tests read demand, conversion, spend and spenders by country, and leave items under their eligibility threshold alone.
- **Prices are read, never typed:** `GetProductInfoAsync` for a product or pass (`GetProductInfo` is deprecated), `GetDeveloperProductsAsync` for every product. `PriceInRobux` is what the viewing account pays; `UserBasePriceInRobux` is the set price. One tester read every product 20% under its set price in two games (349 as 280), one of them with no regional pricing in `priceInformation.enabledFeatures`: read that and compare the two fields before calling it regional pricing. The Dynamic Price Check (the Passes or Developer Products page's menu) finds typed prices: up to five test accounts, each Price pinned (a fixed fake Robux amount) or Location pinned (a fake economic location); enable it, wait a few minutes, play, and disable it after. Managed Pricing on passes sold only on the game's details page needs no check.
- **Product art:** every product's icon is in the game's icon family ([icons.md](../ui/icons.md)), and the same art is the product's own image (`imageFile`: a .jpg, .png or .bmp of at most 512x512, the subject inside the inscribed circle) that Roblox shows on its own surfaces, purchase prompts and the store; none ships with a default or placeholder image.
- **A product named with an amount goes stale when its formula moves:** developer product names stating an amount ("65,000" of the soft currency) were already behind the game's grant (51,000) after an earlier curve change, and the purchase prompt shows Roblox's name, not the game's label, so a buyer is shown more than they get. In a second game, packs sized from the reward curve moved from 63,000 to 65,000 and a bundle's from 76,000 to 78,000 while their product names still said 63,000 and 76,000; the listings were updated through the publish tool's update path (204 each; the art had no numerals). After any change to a formula that sizes products, diff the computed grant text against the published names and descriptions and update them ([product-setup.md](product-setup.md)), or name the products without numbers.
- **A pass whose effect scales with progress is named by what it is, with no number** *(default)*: passes named for a x5 station opened one whose multiplier climbs world by world (x5 to x135); the owner asked for the number to go and the name to end in what the item is, so players are not confused when it grows. Renamed with an Open Cloud PATCH (name and description only; Managed Pricing untouched, read back unchanged). The description can give the range.
- **Present bulk tiers as a per-item price and the percent saved** *(default)*: totals of x1.5 for three and x3 for ten were asked for "a noticeable discount"; the follow-up restated it per item: half the single price for x3 and a quarter for x10, each rounded down to whole Robux per item before multiplying (a 49-Robux item gives 72 and 120).

## Purchases

- **Receipts:** one `ProcessReceipt`, idempotent on `PurchaseId`, returning `PurchaseGranted` only after the save succeeds ([coding.md](../code/coding.md)); a grant that adds currency is logged as an economy event ([game-economy.md](game-economy.md#economy-and-pacing)). Passes are granted on the server's `PromptGamePassPurchaseFinished`; nothing is granted from `PromptProductPurchaseFinished`. A receipt carries only `PurchaseId`, `PlayerId`, `ProductId`, `PlaceIdWherePurchased`, `CurrencySpent`, `CurrencyType` and `ProductPurchaseChannel`; an unresolved one runs again only when the player buys again or rejoins (the player must be in the server for it to run, and a yielding callback has no timeout), and is never refunded. A `PurchaseGranted` return can still fail to be recorded, leaving the purchase to run again, which idempotency absorbs. **One `grant` per product** before launch; once live, change what a product gives by creating a new product id, so a receipt of the old one keeps its meaning. Versioned grants (`grants[n]` plus `grantVersion`) kept only for old receipts are migration code an unreleased game drops ([coding.md](../code/coding.md#data)).
- **Save what the moment decides before prompting:** a pot to double, a revive on the spot, a gift's recipient. Grant from the saved value (a reward kind paying the held amount times the multiplier kept the receipt idempotent and fair), block the free path to the same claim while the prompt is open, and keep such products unlisted (`storePageEnabled` false, which the API never reads back: [product-setup.md](product-setup.md)): a Listed product also sells outside the game, its receipt arriving when the buyer next joins, where no moment exists. A receipt that arrives after its moment still pays: a stored revive, the held pot, a gift credit the buyer sends later.
- **Gifts** *(default)*: everything sold can be bought for a player in the server, and every gift sells at one price everywhere: Managed Pricing stays off on each gift path, so a buyer in a cheap region cannot supply other players. A pass is gifted through its own developer product, which sets an owned flag in the recipient's profile; the pass check reads the flag or `UserOwnsGamePassAsync`. A giftable item whose direct purchase keeps Managed Pricing (a cosmetic) gets its own gift product the same way, Managed Pricing off, so the direct product keeps its regional price while the gift costs the default. Before prompting, check that the recipient does not already own a one-time item and that a paid random item suits both players' policies; a gift path left regionally priced must pass the price-level rule (above). A recipient who left gets it through an inbox key (`UpdateAsync`) claimed on join; acknowledge after that write. Gifts roll luck and are announced like purchases.
- **Announcements** *(default)*: every purchase in the buyer's server, and big ones (top tiers, above a set price) in every server. After each grant the server sends ids (buyer, product, recipient) to its clients, which write the line with `TextChatService.TextChannels.RBXSystem:DisplaySystemMessage` (its second argument, `metadata`, tags the message type): it runs only on the client, shows only to that player and is neither filtered nor localized, so it carries names and the game's own strings, and text a player typed (a gift note) is filtered on the server first (`TextService:FilterStringAsync`, `GetNonChatStringForBroadcastAsync`). Big purchases also go out on one `MessagingService` topic, with `game.JobId` so the sender skips its own copy. A cross-server feed built that way shows each purchase in its own server at once, then publishes it; each server caps the other servers' lines it shows a minute; each line carries a tier tag by price (`[STORE]`, `[RARE]`, `[EPIC]`) and is written by each client from plain fields (display name, the item's catalogue name, price, tier), escaped for rich text. It never publishes from Studio (`RunService:IsStudio()`), so specs that fake receipts cannot reach live servers' chats; live delivery between servers cannot be tested there. Delivery is best-effort. A message holds 1 kB; a server sends at most 600 + 240 × its players messages a minute and holds 20 + 8 × its players subscriptions (240 subscribe requests a minute); the experience receives (400 + 200 × servers) a minute, and one topic (40 + 80 × servers), about 80 broadcasts a minute however many servers run: set the bar for big so global traffic stays under it. Roblox says these limits may change. A failed publish never touches the grant.
- **Lucky purchases** *(default)*: each purchase, gifts included, has a shown chance to start a boost for everyone in the buyer's server, announced there with the buyer's name. Keep it minor and short: a second trigger adds time instead of stacking, and the boost counts in the luck cap and the pacing model ([game-economy.md](game-economy.md#economy-and-pacing)). It is a paid random item: show the chance with the products, and a buyer whose `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted` is true gets no roll, or a fixed share of a shown server boost meter.

## Paid random items

The [policy](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items) covers anything random bought with Robux *or with currency Robux can buy* (rolls, eggs, spins, capsules, rerolls, random enhancements, fusions, lucky-purchase rolls), plus the luck boosts and pity that change its odds. Before the spend, show every outcome's odds as percentages summing to 100% (a list behind a labeled button, not a bare (i); rounded values say they are rounded); explain boosts with numbers and keep the shown odds live while one is active; update odds when outcomes run out. When `PolicyService:GetPolicyInfoForPlayerAsync` reports `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted`, give that player one of the policy's alternatives (a free earnable path, a fixed disclosed order, direct purchase at the expected value) or hide the item, block its purchase with a message, or keep the player out of the parts of the game that sell it. Random rewards earned free need no odds. Trading paid items is in [game-economy.md](game-economy.md#economy-and-pacing). A game whose roll currency can be bought falls under this policy: once a game sells its soft currency, every egg or roll bought with it needs the gate, not only those sold for Robux. Show each outcome's odds in the egg's own window before any spend, and count a policy read that fails on a live server as restricted (in Studio, as unrestricted, so the eggs can be tested). Before selling one, check that its worst outcome has a use, and stop offering it once it can only give duplicates *(default)*.
