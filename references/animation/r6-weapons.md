# R6 weapon clips: swings, idles and carries

For a held weapon on a fake-R6 (or R6) rig, beside [r6-on-r15.md](r6-on-r15.md)'s joint policy. The weapon's own joint and grip: [gear.md](../rigs/gear.md).

## The shape

- **Swings** (a user's R6 slashes that read well, measured): a key every 1 to 3 frames (25 to 40 keys in 0.7 to 1.0 s, all Linear); the torso twisting 50 to 78 degrees each way with the head counter-turning 60 to 80 to hold the target; the arms through near-full arcs with 1 to 3 studs of slide for reach; the weapon's own Motor6D keyed for 30 to 60 degrees of wrist-like flick; legs and root left to the clip beneath. Clips of 6 to 13 eased keys with a modest torso and a welded weapon read stiff even with their amplitudes doubled. Slide along the arm's line, as theirs do (mean |cos| 0.64 to 0.88): slid across a hanging arm, the block floated before the shins. Key the flick with `keyHeld` (a Blender clip's `Flick` channel, `clip_import.py --held`), its joint frame's +Y along the blade, measured to the tip: R15's `RightGripAttachment` is turned -90 degrees about X, so a frame derived from it ran along the arm. A flick near -85 lays the blade along the arm, so a thrust's point leads, which R6's square grip cannot. A swing exported as a layer (`parts`) leaves a moving player's walk its legs.
- **The weapon carries the swing, not only the arm** *(default; a user's direction)*: the weapon's Motor6D is a joint to animate in full, as an animator keys a sword's own Motor6D. Draw the blade through the cut's whole arc but swing the rigid arm only part of it (half to three quarters of its way out from the cut's middle), and key the joint (`keyHeld`'s `Flick`, past 90 when the blade swings beyond the arm's line, and `Twist` about the arm) to point the blade the rest of the way; keep the arm's roll holding the blade as R6's grip would, and let the joint make up the difference, which then stays in the cut's plane as flick. Turning the arm from hanging with no roll instead flipped it over the head (an arm near straight up has no single shortest swing: 60 degrees in a frame) and twisted the joint through whole turns. Keep each joint under about 40 degrees a frame (a wide trailing cut pulled to half its arc whipped the joint 47 a frame: give it three quarters; a leading, rising cut takes more). Idles and walks key the joint too, the point swaying a beat behind the body, so no clip rides the weapon welded.
- **A swing must visibly travel** *(default)*: a readable coil with a short charge (about 4 frames longer than a whip), a cut of about 5 frames with the weapon joint flicked out (-55 to -62) so the blade extends from the arm, and a follow-through past the target that drops the arm. Never hold a raised finish (2-3 frame whips into an arm held up or out for 0.17-0.25 s read as the sword being held up), and never recover through an upright-blade waypoint. Where a string stops, the finish may hold 0.17-0.25 s, drifting rather than frozen, with the arm down; a chained hit cuts into that hold, so the Hit frames stay as they are.
- **Weapon idle** *(default)*: the torso near upright (pitched -2 to -4.5 degrees), a shallow crouch (0.08 to 0.18 studs) and a level head; 8 to 12.5 degrees of forward pitch with a 0.15-0.31 stud crouch and a nodding head read as hunched. The string's guard pose follows the idle.

## Aim

- **Measure where each blow lands; do not estimate it.** For any cut that turns the body, read the blade tip's heading on the Hit marker (and where its fastest sweep falls) from the review blade every frame: an estimate from the trail's angle missed by two frames, and a spinning cut's Hit fell with the tip 54 degrees to the side. Time it so the tip crosses dead ahead on the Hit.
- **A string: tabulate every hit's heading before re-timing one.** When the user names "the fourth swing", they may count differently: a table of all the hits' headings at their blows shows the outlier at once (one at +43 among -22, -34, +3, -6).
- **A one-handed overhead chop off the weapon-side shoulder** lands on the centre line only with the torso turned about 15 degrees toward the other side and the arm's cutting plane angled in (its end direction 0.05 inward): with the torso turned toward the weapon side, the blow landed 43 degrees off; corrected, at -6 degrees, 6.7 studs ahead.

## Carries and clearance

- **Lifting a hanging item's tip: scan both ways.** A blade hanging near straight down passes through vertical under a flick, so +30 raised the tip 0.9 studs and -30 lowered it; +55 cleared the floor but swung the blade across the legs, and going the other way past vertical (about -80) cleared it trailing behind. Scan a few flick values with a preview before settling a carry.
- **Check the held tip against the floor in every clip that carries it:** name the blade's tip attachment (`clip_import.py --tip`, AnimationInspector's `Tip`): the tip line gives its lowest height over the floor and `tip-floor` warns under it. A walk carry trailing a long blade ran its point 1.75 to 2.12 studs under the floor through the whole loop and passed the Blender audit, the import's policy check and AnimationInspector with 0 findings.
