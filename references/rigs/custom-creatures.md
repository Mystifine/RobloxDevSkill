# Custom creatures and articulated assets

1. **Model it to its references first,** through [modeling.md](../art/modeling.md)'s Workflow: list each reference's silhouette, mass and armour, then judge clay renders beside them, all before rigging. A slender generic dragon was sent back as looking very different from its references; rebuilt with their bulk (deep chest, thick neck), banded throat and belly, swept crest blades, rows of teeth and plated wings, it was accepted. A region with a visible colour edge (a belly, a chest plate) is its own piece sunk into the body: painted by face after a voxel remesh and decimation, the edge stair-stepped and leaked onto the legs, where a sunk ellipsoid gave a smooth curve.
2. **Plan for the intended action.** Fit joints to anatomy and motion: spine/neck/jaw, limb chains, tail, wings or mechanical axes as applicable. Do not add unused controls or force R6/R15 naming onto a custom skeleton. Decide root placement, scale, ground contact and any animation compatibility requirements before modeling joints.
3. **Build a functional rig.** For organic bending, bind the mesh to a deform skeleton with normalized weights, at most four influences per vertex, no unweighted vertices or stray distant influences. Place enough topology at bending joints. For rigid plates, horns, eyes, claws and mechanical pieces, use appropriate rigid weights or joints/attachments so they follow their intended parent without stretching or detaching: bound wholly to that bone, never weighted by distance, since crest blades reaching back over the neck lie nearer the neck's bones and bend with it. Parts spanning moving joints need intentional splitting or deformation.
4. **Choose runtime control for the project.** Keep a Humanoid when movement or other project systems depend on it; otherwise use an AnimationController with an Animator for skeletal playback. Use `Animator:LoadAnimation` for runtime tracks, not the deprecated AnimationController proxy. Do not apply the standard-rig `RigAudit.controller` helper blindly to custom anatomy. Set root anchoring and collision for the intended controller; do not anchor every independently moving part.
5. **Stress-test before final baking.** Pose the major moving chains through representative ranges, including shoulder/hip bends, torso turns, jaw opening and appendage motion where present. Inspect pinching, collapsing volume, detached details, gaps exposed by motion and armor intersections. Restore the intended rest pose before export.
6. **Export and re-import the actual rig.** When available, use `blender-character-rigging` -> `blender-animation` for diagnostic motion -> `blender-export-pipelines`. Bake authoring controls to the export skeleton where necessary; verify bones/joints, names, hierarchy, weights and rigid attachments survived. Name bones apart from mesh objects: a bone named like one (Body, Door) came back from the Open Cloud importer as that MeshPart on a Motor6D, which clips must pose as a joint ([blender.md](../animation/blender.md#into-studio)). In Studio, use the Animation Editor or a short diagnostic clip to verify that the major chains actually drive the imported geometry. Looking correct in Blender, counting bones, or inspecting a rest pose is insufficient. Diagnostic verification does not require publishing a production animation asset.
7. **Verify collision and delivery.** Bone deformation changes appearance, not collision geometry; configure suitable root/collision proxies separately and verify contact where needed. Check the actor's orientation, pivot and ground contact, plus visible deformation at gameplay distance. Standard R6/R15 RigAudit/RigSkin rules do not validate arbitrary creature skeletons; use applicable mesh/weight checks and explicit pose inspection instead. AnimationInspector can help with supported clips, with custom feet/hinges configured and its coverage checked; a skipped or unmatched channel is not a pass.

## Shapes that read

- **A small creature's feathered wing is an oval with a scalloped trailing edge,** not fanned feathers: five feather capsules fanned from the shoulder (with a covert lobe on top) read as a spread hand with fingers on six stylized pets in review renders, and still did with a web filling the fan to 62% of each feather's length. An oval wing body, a shoulder lobe, a row of round feather tips sunk a third into its lower edge and one longer primary at the tip read as wings at full size and at 128 px. Judge a wing by its silhouette at game distance.

## Import and clips

- **Stand an imported FBX model's pivot upright before moving it by its pivot:** 23 rigged pets uploaded through Open Cloud as Models and loaded with `InsertService:LoadAsset` had upright meshes (`UpVector` 0, 1, 0) but a PrimaryPart whose `PivotOffset` was turned 90 degrees about X, so `GetPivot()` read (90, 0, 0), and the follower's `PivotTo` an upright frame laid every pet on its side (mesh `UpVector` 0, 0, 1), with or without clips playing. Setting `WorldPivot` changed nothing while a PrimaryPart was set ([rigs.md](rigs.md), step 5); `PrimaryPart.PivotOffset = PrimaryPart.CFrame:ToObjectSpace(uprightGroundPivot)` stood them all up. An Edit check that moved the raw model with `PivotTo(CFrame.new(...))` had hidden it by turning the model upright once: check orientation without moving the model by its pivot.
- **Mirror checks pair every side-suffix form:** a weight-symmetry check that flipped only names ending in `_L`/`_R` reported a 1.00 asymmetry on a bat with two-segment wings (`Wing_L2`) and on a quadruped (`Leg_BL`): the flip left those names unchanged, so mirrored vertices never compared like with like. Matching `_<optional letter><L|R><optional digits>` brought both to 0.0.
- **Never give a mesh a bone's name in an FBX for Roblox:** the import turned a bone named like a mesh (`Body`) into that mesh's part on a Motor6D, so the rig lost the bone and the build failed.
- **Solve planted multi-bone limbs every couple of frames:** tentacles and folded legs keyed as sparse bone turns sagged up to 0.5 studs through the floor between keys, each bone interpolating alone; key the parameters (the body's move, each tip's target) and re-solve the chain (FABRIK with a minimum knee height) every 2 frames.
- **Probe floor contact on every keyed frame,** not a sample: a pipeline checking every 6th frame passed clips whose 4th frame put a limb 0.2 studs through the floor.
- **A body that rises from or sinks into a floor opening:** test where its surface crosses the floor plane outside the opening's radius (and the rim that hides a band), not its lowest point, which flags it everywhere.
- **Let hanging pieces hang:** a prop weighted rigidly to a moving tip swings through the floor; turn it plumb from the tip's live placement each frame and tilt it out just enough (its length and half width) to rest on the floor.

Do not call the actor animation-ready until its imported rig has passed the motion/deformation check. If Studio verification is unavailable, preserve the rigged source/export and state that the imported result is unverified; do not silently deliver a static model as complete. Report which chains and poses were checked and any remaining limitations. Rig-only tasks do not need the publishing/full-clip workflow in [animation.md](../animation/animation.md).
