# Custom creatures and articulated assets

1. **Plan for the intended action.** Fit joints to anatomy and motion: spine/neck/jaw, limb chains, tail, wings or mechanical axes as applicable. Do not add unused controls or force R6/R15 naming onto a custom skeleton. Decide root placement, scale, ground contact and any animation compatibility requirements before modeling joints.
2. **Build a functional rig.** For organic bending, bind the mesh to a deform skeleton with normalized weights, at most four influences per vertex, no unweighted vertices or stray distant influences. Place enough topology at bending joints. For rigid plates, horns, eyes, claws and mechanical pieces, use appropriate rigid weights or joints/attachments so they follow their intended parent without stretching or detaching. Parts spanning moving joints need intentional splitting or deformation.
3. **Choose runtime control for the project.** Keep a Humanoid when movement or other project systems depend on it; otherwise use an AnimationController with an Animator for skeletal playback. Use `Animator:LoadAnimation` for runtime tracks, not the deprecated AnimationController proxy. Do not apply the standard-rig `RigAudit.controller` helper blindly to custom anatomy. Set root anchoring and collision for the intended controller; do not anchor every independently moving part.
4. **Stress-test before final baking.** Pose the major moving chains through representative ranges, including shoulder/hip bends, torso turns, jaw opening and appendage motion where present. Inspect pinching, collapsing volume, detached details, gaps exposed by motion and armor intersections. Restore the intended rest pose before export.
5. **Export and re-import the actual rig.** When available, use `blender-character-rigging` -> `blender-animation` for diagnostic motion -> `blender-export-pipelines`. Bake authoring controls to the export skeleton where necessary; verify bones/joints, names, hierarchy, weights and rigid attachments survived. In Studio, use the Animation Editor or a short diagnostic clip to verify that the major chains actually drive the imported geometry. Looking correct in Blender, counting bones, or inspecting a rest pose is insufficient. Diagnostic verification does not require publishing a production animation asset.
6. **Verify collision and delivery.** Bone deformation changes appearance, not collision geometry; configure suitable root/collision proxies separately and verify contact where needed. Check the actor's orientation, pivot and ground contact, plus visible deformation at gameplay distance. Standard R6/R15 RigAudit/RigSkin rules do not validate arbitrary creature skeletons; use applicable mesh/weight checks and explicit pose inspection instead. AnimationInspector can help with supported clips, with custom feet/hinges configured and its coverage checked; a skipped or unmatched channel is not a pass.

Do not call the actor animation-ready until its imported rig has passed the motion/deformation check. If Studio verification is unavailable, preserve the rigged source/export and state that the imported result is unverified; do not silently deliver a static model as complete. Report which chains and poses were checked and any remaining limitations. Rig-only tasks do not need the publishing/full-clip workflow in [animation.md](../animation/animation.md).

Roblox references: [rigging and skinning](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/rigging), [Animator](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Animator).
