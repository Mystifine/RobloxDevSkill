# Cutscenes and cinematic cameras

A cutscene is a timeline: camera, clips, effects and sound keyed to one clock. Build it so any second can be reproduced, since that is the only way to check it. Effects inside it still pass [vfx.md](vfx.md); poses follow the animation route. A scene with spoken lines adds [captions.md](captions.md).

## From a reference
- **Build to its numbers:** watch it with [video.md](video.md) and build each shot to what it records: length, holds, beats, size, where the subject sits and faces, camera move and lens. Check your recording against the reference with `compare`, shot by shot, not only by timing.
- **Record a cold first play in real time,** the first in a fresh Play session: held frames of a warm session all looked right, while the first real play showed the opening set black and a held blade missing for a second as assets loaded, and the same compare found six more missing beats that a vague "missing a little" pointed at. Preload the cutscene's props (`ContentProvider:PreloadAsync`) before it can start.
- **A length ceiling, never a target** *(default)*: a scene is as long as its content. Per-tier length targets (12-15 s for the commonest tier, up to 22-28 s for the rarest) made builders pad short moments with near-static holds of 2.4 and 4.2 s. Find padding as the longest stretches where nothing new starts: list every cut, burst, sound hit, prop entrance and move by the other side, then look at each long gap, since a deliberate slow beat (a charge) shows as one too.
- **List what is left out, and why:** a rule (all ages, any avatar) or something the cutscene cannot do yet (a focus pull, a bloom pulse). The second is a feature to raise with the user, never a silent drop.
- **An object the reference is known by** (a weapon, a creature) that the project lacks: build it (weapons at the gear budget, [gear.md](../rigs/gear.md)) or ask at the start. Listed as left out at the end, the shots, pose and timing had to be reworked around it.
- **A fast strike has no visible travel** *(default)*: a 0.8 s dash down a 28-stud lane, true to its reference's timing, read as a slow slide. Move the body in one frame under two to six flash frames, then show what the speed left behind (a line in the air, the floor erupting along the path, a skid). Only the root jumps in that frame: the joints still take 4 or more frames ([animation.md](../animation/animation.md), `pop`).

## Framing
- **Shots that must work on any avatar frame the body,** waist-up or wider, anchored to the root or torso. Close-ups anchored to `Head` or a hand showed an empty head under a floating crown (a headless avatar) or a wall of grey blocks; waist-up and medium shots of the same moments read on every avatar. Face close-ups are for rigs you control.
- **Frame by the body's measured size and the pose it holds,** never by fixed offsets: a milestone cutscene with fixed camera offsets put the camera under the chin of a large form, dropped a curled gathering pose (the head at about hip height) onto the letterbox bar, and sat the title card over the head in the reveal. Offsets scaled by the head-top height, aimed lower for the curled shot, with the reveal framed low so the card reads above the head, worked for a small and a large form. A held clock (below) made each shot capturable, though the server's body swap still ran on real time.
- **No camera down a blocky R15 arm:** the default hand is a 0.98 x 0.32 x 1.03 slab, so a camera behind the shoulder, on the line through the fist, saw the back of the torso, and raised to head height a wall of hand. Reframe around what the avatar can show first. When a shot over the hand is truly wanted, show an unattached stand-in hand of Parts in the camera's frame each frame, with the real arm, head and accessories at `LocalTransparencyModifier` 1 for that window: it frames the same on any avatar. Place it by reading the posed parts' positions and solving the offset that puts the target on the camera's ray at a set depth; hand-tuned offsets missed repeatedly.
- **Particles fill a near camera:** effects sized in studs fill the frame of a camera about 8 studs away, and smoke washes it from inside. Light a character's aura after any shot whose camera sits within a few studs of the body (an over-the-shoulder shot 2 studs behind the head washed pink, and the hit after it whited out), and size each effect for the closest shot that shows it. Size a prop for the closest camera that shows it, the moment it leaves a hand included: an orb near full size 0.1 s after launch filled half a shot 7 studs away, and keyed as a small spark until the next cut it read. A burst before a cut reaches the next shot's lens too, by its speed over its drag plus its largest particle: one 13 to 15 studs from the next camera (particles up to 35 studs, moving 20 to 30 studs a second) filled it with fog, and 5 to 8 studs further off at 0.6 to 0.7 of its size it cleared. Keep about 20 studs or shrink it, and film the result.
- **Find a wash-out by its time,** not by guessing the largest effect: line stills up against the keyed effect times. A haze blamed on expanding rings was there before the first ring fired; successive stills matched a small particle star, then swirl bursts.
- **Judge a fight shot at the camera's real distance,** from held frames at windup and impact in each shot: too close, the body filled the frame and the surface being hit was out of shot; too far, the arms were lost against a pale background.
- **A low shot of something high:** a long lens from far away. 30 studs back at FOV 40 looked steeply up, and a ledge hid the figure standing on it; 70 studs back at FOV 25 matched the reference and kept the figure clear.
- **A flat prop** (a disc, ring, blade or card) **is a line edge-on:** choose each shot by its face's normal, or give it thickness. A crescent made as a thin disc vanished from in front and from behind, the two obvious cameras for something thrown down a lane, and read at once from above while level and from three quarters while upright.
- **Keep the camera off a lit Neon floor:** 0.5 studs above a glowing pad the frame was white edge to edge; 1.7 studs up it was clean. Raise it or tilt it away.
- **A black or solid card inside a 3D scene:** a grade cannot black out the picture and keep particles glowing. A hollow box of black Neon parts round the camera (Neon is unlit, so it reads pure black) with the particles inside, far from the stage so nobody else sees it.
- **Grading a sky to a colour it lacks:** [environment.md](environment.md), Post-processing.
- **A close-up on a face in contact goes on the side away from the contact, at or under that face's eye line:** two fighters locked fist to palm, the taller looking 12 degrees down at the other. From the contact side at shoulder height the fists crossed his face (the sight line 0.65 studs over fists about 0.5 across); in profile his own reaching arm and his side hair covered it; from 11 degrees above, his fringe and a mask worn on the brow covered the eyes, only the top of the head showing. From the far side at the other fighter's shoulder height (0.2 studs under his eyes) the whole face read. Counter the head against the torso's lean first in any pose meant to show a face (a nod of -8 to -16 degrees against 14 to 30 degrees of lean): with the lean alone both were bowed heads in every shot.
- **Turn a camera by blending its look directions, never by sliding its look point between a far and a near target:** a pan from the sky (a point 170 studs off) back to a body 7 studs away, made by lerping the look point, left the body out of the picture for about a second (frames showed only scenery) and whipped onto it at the end, since nearly all the angular change falls in the last tenth of the lerp. Blending the two unit directions from the camera's position over 0.7 s, then travelling, kept the body in every frame.
- **On a tall screen, widen the lens rather than move the camera:** `Camera.FieldOfView` is vertical, so on a 401 x 777 portrait phone a 50 degree profile shot of two bodies 16 studs apart showed neither (8.7 studs of half-width where 9.5 were needed). Widening the field of view by 1.4 / aspect, capped at 104 degrees, with the viewpoint unchanged (so no new occlusion or clipping), put both in the picture; landscape phones and desktops stay at 50 and a 4:3 tablet gets 52. Check a portrait phone in the device simulator on a held frame: `StudioDeviceSimulatorService` setters work from the Play client mid-session, and `SetOrientationAsync` errors while a desktop preset is active ("orientation can only be set on mobile"), so set the device first.

## Characters in a cutscene
- **Hold the player still from the server, and freeze input with a `ContextActionService` sink,** for any scene the player cannot skip (a milestone, a transformation). Disabling the client's PlayerModule controls alone let the player move during one. Walk speed and jump set to 0 in the server's single stat-applying function while the scene runs (with the client's controls re-disabled every 0.25 s, later replaced by the sink below) gave 0.00 studs of drift with W, D and Space pressed; damage refused in the damage function (not only a `ForceField`) left health 105 -> 105 under a 40-damage hit; both came back when the scene ended (walk 28, jump 7.2) and the player walked away. On the client, never wait for a PlayerModule: a place whose `PlayerScripts` held only `RbxCharacterSounds` and the game's client had none, so the scene's `WaitForChild("PlayerModule", 5)` returned nil after 5 s, twice per scene. It began 10.9 s after the press (the server swapped the body at 3.95 s), and the same call made from the scene's RenderStep step left the camera `Scriptable` at FOV 64 sixteen seconds later; the user saw a long delay and a stuck camera. Bind a `ContextActionService` action at high priority that returns `Enum.ContextActionResult.Sink` for every `Enum.PlayerActions` item, and unbind it when the scene ends: the scene then took the camera 0.64 s after the probe was armed and handed it back (`Custom`, FOV 70) at 9.04 s. Check `PlayerScripts` for a PlayerModule before relying on one.
- A character the cutscene lifts by CFrame needs Freefall disabled meanwhile ([gameplay.md](../code/gameplay.md)); its aura rides with `LockedToPart` ([vfx.md](vfx.md)).
- Start the cutscene the way the game does (its prompt, remote or UI), not by requiring its modules from `execute_luau`. While you iterate, a self-contained timeline required there replays any one outcome on demand ([studio-testing.md](../code/studio-testing.md#studio-mcp-calls)).
- **No added body details by default** *(default)*: temporary fingers or glowing eyes built to match a reference's close-ups were removed as not needed, so offer them as a choice. When fingers are wanted, build them from Parts (SmoothPlastic, the avatar's colours): a skinned mesh hand worked and was turned down as unsuited to Roblox avatars.
- **A held prop rides a Weld** from the hand to the prop's primary part, with `C1` its `PivotOffset` and `C0` keyed per beat: an anchored prop placed every frame trails an animated hand by one frame.
- **Anything that meets the body** (a ball to a hand, a blow to a target) is keyed in that part's frame, not where the clip's numbers put it: placed by the numbers, a ball met nothing, since the swing landed 0.19 s early on an unheld snap ease and 2 studs low. Printing the part's position at each keyed beat in Play found both faults in one run; keyed to the hand's frame, the ball met it on every avatar size.
- **A move off a raised floor includes the drop:** a target thrown 22 studs past its pad kept the pad's height and hovered 0.95 studs over the real ground. Raycast the floor where it lands, and check a lying or kneeling body by its parts' corners against it, allowing for the avatar's root sitting lower than the clip rig's (0.19 studs).
- **A character stays opaque:** held partly transparent, a model of overlapping pieces shows them through one another (a summoned skinned figure at 8 to 15% for its last two seconds looked washed out). Fade it in or out within a few frames (0.06 to 0.08 s), under a burst.
- **Clones standing in for players** (a local cutscene's cast): move the hidden real character far off (400 studs up) for the scene, since rays that find an actor's floor hit it: hidden and anchored under its stand-in, it lifted the whole scene, camera and props included, by 2.3 studs. Strip BillboardGuis (names, titles) copied with the clone. **A stand-in with a Humanoid stays collidable at the torso:** a cloned character with every part set `CanCollide`, `CanQuery` and `CanTouch` false and `Humanoid.EvaluateStateMachine` false read `UpperTorso` and `LowerTorso` `CanCollide` true 20 heartbeats after parenting (`CanQuery` still false, which is ignored while `CanCollide` is true), and a `RespectCanCollide` ray from above hit `UpperTorso` at 3.61 studs. Effects that find the floor with such a ray (expanding rings, a scorch disc) drew on the stand-ins' chests: rings floated at chest height in every fight shot, and an 11-stud dark disc hung 2 studs over the landing, so a low camera 6 studs away looked up through it and three quarters of the picture went dark for a second. It was misread first as smoke, then as a map dome; the sign was the straight polygon edge of a cylinder seen from below. Adding the stand-ins' folder to the ray's ignore list fixed all of it: name the stand-ins to every effect, camera and ground ray.
- **Hand the camera back** with `CameraType = Custom` and `CameraSubject = the current Humanoid` (the character's now, not the one the camera left), every time: a character that respawned while the camera was Scriptable kept the removed Humanoid as its subject, and Custom alone left the camera stranded where the body died. Test a respawn during every scripted camera.

## Check it frame by frame
- **One clock:** every keyed value (camera, poses, effects, text) reads the seconds since the cutscene started, never its own `task.wait` chain.
- **Measure and film on a second play:** a newly uploaded clip's first play started late (a beat sampled the pose before it), and a new mesh was missing from a film taken 8 s into Play while a background preload ran. The cold first play is a check of its own ([From a reference](#from-a-reference)).
- **Hold a second for captures,** built in before the first review of any long scripted sequence: a workspace number attribute, read only when `RunService:IsStudio()`, overrides the clock, fires every one-shot event up to that second and holds every keyed value there. `screen_capture` lands about 1.2 s after the call, so waiting for a moment and then capturing misses every fast beat; held, each frame came out exact. **Choose a shot's camera live on the held second,** never by arithmetic: one close-up cost three full build, record and review cycles (about ten minutes each) on framings reasoned from coordinates, all of which failed. With the scene held on that second, a `RenderStep` bound after the scene's own set the camera from a table of candidates switched by an attribute, and one capture per candidate found the working framing in four captures and a few minutes.
- **Jump to a second:** a temporary LocalScript harness starts the cutscene from second T with its clips frozen at T, for repeatable frames and printed positions. Captures taken while effects play can time out or return stale frames. Wait for `track.Length > 0` before setting `TimePosition`: set sooner on a new clip, it froze at 0 (held frames showed the opening pose at 1.69 s); wait with `while track.Length == 0 do task.wait() end`. Stop the previous run fully before the next, since an overlapping stop can clear state they share, such as captions.
- **A harness that needs the game's own modules** (its state, its UI) runs as a LocalScript: [studio-testing.md](../code/studio-testing.md).
- **Judging placement** *(default)*: give each limb its own colour (on the client only), turn the grade and outlines off, and print the parts' screen positions with `Camera:WorldToViewportPoint`. Under a tinted grade a torso read as lime, and an arm behind a headless avatar's missing head read as its head (a headless avatar draws no head); with colour-coded limbs, two crossed arms and an over-raised reach showed at once. A diagnostic camera goes in as a temporary shot added to the cutscene's live shot table: one set in `BindToRenderStep` at `Last` priority was overwritten before the capture read the frame.
- **A head for clearance:** a headless avatar draws no head, so films hide every prop that passes through it. Give a test character a temporary head (about 1.2 studs, on the client, never saved) before filming, and list what overlaps it each frame (`GetPartsInPart`): films showed nothing, and the probe found blades inside the head for seconds in four scenes. Measure in the prop's own frame against where the body ends up, not where it starts: a swing trace keyed at the cut sat 0.95 studs into a lunging caster's head, and 2 studs further ahead it cleared.
- **Rewinding keeps what a later moment made:** held at 3.2 s, then at 0.6 s, the scene still showed an aura and eyes that first appear at 1.5 and 2.4 s. Restart the cutscene before checking an earlier moment.
- **A keyed value runs from the previous key that sets it,** so give every fade a hold key where it should start. A prop keyed Fade 0 at the start and Fade 1 at 1.43 s faded through its whole flight and read as a ghost before the hit; the logs and timings were all correct, and only a frame burst of the recording showed it.
- Single effects inside it: `Inspector.hold` ([vfx.md](vfx.md) section 8).
