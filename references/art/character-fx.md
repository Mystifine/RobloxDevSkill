# Character effects

Effects on and around a character and the moments they mark: always-on auras, transformations, payoffs that fly to the player, and the camera kicks that go with them. Read from [vfx.md](vfx.md), whose rules apply to every layer.

## Auras
- **Where an aura emits:**
  1. A body-sized host (an invisible part welded round an R15 body, 3.2 x 5.2 x 3.2 studs) with `Shape` `Cylinder`, `ShapeStyle` `Surface` and `ShapeInOut` `Outward` showed almost nothing in Play captures: no visible flames at `Rate` 30 or 60 with `Brightness` 3, a faint haze at 150. Switching only the shape to `Box` with `ShapeStyle` `Volume` made the flames appear at once.
  2. A box round the whole body then read as a cloud, and emitters on each R15 limb's own surface showed only at the shoulders of a hooded, cloaked form: the clothes and cloak covered every other limb surface.
  3. **What worked, for clothed or armoured characters:** an invisible shell per limb (the limb's size x 1.3 plus 0.3 studs a side, welded to it) carrying the emitters put flames, stars and orbs on the shoulders, arms, waist and legs, outside the clothes. `VFXInspector` lint: 0 findings on all seven auras (18-21 emitters, a light and 10 shells each).
- **`Brightness` above 1 under graded light:** the built-in `rbxasset` textures (`fire_main`, `sparkles_main`, `smoke_main`) read as dim brown under a world's dusk colour grade until `Brightness` was 3-5. After the change (volume emission, `Brightness` 1.5-5 by style, 1.5x the rate, 1.35x the size) the fire, spark, wisp and mist styles all read in captures.
- **Colours:** flames fading into the aura's darkest red read as brown smoke in a sunny lobby; fade flame layers through their hot colours only (core and middle). A pale-core wisp aura read as white specks until its orbs were born in its saturated colour and grew 1.3x.
- **Flame tongues go behind the body:** a flipbook of flame crowns (4 x 4, `FacingCameraWorldUp`, emitted from each limb's shell, rising at 2.5-4.5 studs/s, size 0.6 to 2.1 to 1.1 studs) turned soft auras into anime power-up flames. In front of the body the tongues buried it and the pale first colour washed them to white; with `ZOffset` -1.4 x the body scale they rendered behind the body and framed its outline, the body stayed readable, and their colours ran from the aura's middle colour to its tip. A rainbow aura's tongues in a two-colour gradient came out pastel; its full five-colour sequence kept the rainbow. Lightning from a branching flipbook bolt (lifetime 0.16-0.26 s, `Brightness` 6, white into the aura colour) and a whirl lying flat under the feet (`VelocityPerpendicular`, `RotSpeed` 140-200) completed the set, every texture from the game's existing effects pack.
- **Judge an aura in Play, on a character:** of seven auras built on review rigs in Edit, one rig's captures showed only the floor ring and the core glow, though its flame-tongue, orb and streak emitters existed, were enabled and had normal rates, and still did with every other rig's emitters off (169 emitters left in the workspace). Other rigs in the same row drew theirs, and the same aura on a player in Play drew every layer, so Edit cannot give an aura's verdict.
- **Tint a textured MeshPart with a Highlight, never its part `Color`:** a rare variant's tint set as a pet MeshPart's `Color` (#C3A0B6) changed nothing on screen, since the pets were MeshParts drawing a `TextureID` with no SurfaceAppearance (0 surfaces to tint). A Highlight (a pastel fill at 0.72 transparency, shimmering by 0.14, `DepthMode` `Occluded`) showed the shift on the follower; mind the 31 Highlights a client draws at once. A SurfaceAppearance takes its own `Color`. In UI a ViewportFrame takes the tint through its `ImageColor3` (Highlights and particles do not draw there), and a 2D icon through its ImageLabel's `ImageColor3`.
- **A pack texture swapped in without its flipbook layout draws as squares:** set the pack's `FlipbookLayout` with it ([vfx-packs.md](vfx-packs.md)).

## Transformations
**A gather, then a burst, with the body swap hidden in the burst's flash.** An evolution effect that placed pack templates (a vortex blast, a shockwave, a charge aura) at an identity CFrame on the floor drew sideways: a template's authored axes need not be vertical, so check its orientation before placing it on the floor, or build the floor layers procedurally, laid flat. The sequence that worked: a rune circle of flat rings and runes turning faster, sparks spiralling into the body, a building light and a riser sound, the camera leaning in; at 1.23 s a white body highlight, a pillar, two flat shockwave rings, a `ForceField` dome, sparks falling from the pillar, a screen flash and a camera kick; then the title card. With the server's body swap delayed to 1.25 s, three captures showed the old body gathering, the swap hidden in the flash and the new form revealed, nothing sideways.

## Payoffs that fly to a character
- **Time a server move after the client effect's peak at that character:** a pad claim's client sequence spiralled orbs into the character and burst at 0.95 s, but the server sent the player home at 0.85 s, so the burst, dome and flash played at the base after the teleport and orbs homing on the root streaked across the map. The sign: every capture of the claim showed the player already home, with only the count-up banner. Compare each server-driven move (teleport, despawn, respawn) with the effect timeline that plays at that character: raising the server delay to 1.6 s (still inside the claim's cooldown and the map's quiet time) let captures show the pillar at 0.3-0.6 s and the burst at about 1 s, on the pad.
- **An effect that crosses a map is big, streaked and slow on arrival:** orbs flown from a well 196 studs away to the player in the lobby were invisible for most of the arc with 0.9-stud cores and 0.35 s trails. At 1.7x the size with trails 1.8x as long, easing out (leaving fast, arriving slow, winding once round the body), a capture slowed with the game's slow-motion attribute showed a clear streak coming through the gate to the player.

## Camera kicks
**One owner for the field of view, summing kicks as offsets over a fixed rest; shakes go through it too.** Spamming level-ups or evolutions left the camera zoomed: each effect tweened `FieldOfView` to a target and back to whatever the camera showed when it began, so a kick started inside another's lean took that lean as its rest, and the camera settled off 70. One owner that keeps a list of kicks (offset, rise, fall), writes the rest plus the sum of live offsets each frame while any is live, and puts the rest back exactly when the last one ends, plus a hold for cutscenes, brought twelve overlapping kicks fired in 0.4 s back to exactly 70. Never tween back to the current field of view as a rest.
