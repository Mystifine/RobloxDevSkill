# Audio: sound effects, music and mixing

An agent cannot hear, so every choice here is made from numbers: what a file contains, when it peaks and how loud the mix gets. Toolbox rules for sounds: [assets.md](../safety/assets.md).

## Find
- **Any public Toolbox sound may be used** *(default)*: it is published on the Creator Store and has passed Roblox's moderation, so no further licence check is needed. A sound Roblox later removes loads silent, so keep every id in the project's sound table, where one can be swapped.
- **Search the licensed libraries first,** whose catalogs are large and well described: Pro Sound Effects (userId `7462895450`) and APM (userId `7462718749`). Their audio plays in any experience; audio a creator uploaded but did not publish is often private to their own.
- **Search them through the Toolbox API**, since Studio MCP search cannot filter by creator: `GET https://apis.roblox.com/toolbox-service/v1/marketplace/3?keyword=<words>&creatorTargetId=<userId>&creatorType=1&limit=<n>` (3 is audio) lists ids, and `GET https://apis.roblox.com/toolbox-service/v1/items/details?assetIds=<id,...>` gives each one's title, artist, `audioType` (`SoundEffect` or `Music`), category, duration and description, which confirms the publisher. The fields sit under `asset.audioDetails` (`audioType`, title, artist, album, tags) and `asset.duration`. Both answer with no key (read through a web fetch tool); `creatorType` is 1 for a user and 2 for a group, and the search pages by cursor with `limit` up to 100.
- **Search by the libraries' own category words:** searches for game words came back full of rips of commercial games' sounds, which are skipped *(default)*; the licensed libraries' own category words (Cartoon, User Interface, Pops, Stings, Energy) found far more. Roblox's own account (userId 1) publishes first-party UI packs ("Roblox GUI - *", "Roblox_UI_*").
- **Lists of ids from the DevForum:** its pages came back empty through a web fetch tool, while a topic's `/t/<id>.json` and `/search.json?q=<words>` worked through curl.
- **Music: keep only real tracks.** A music search filtered to APM returned many short entries typed `SoundEffect` (6-15 s stingers; "Sworn Enemies D" at 7 s). Keeping `audioType` `Music` and 85 s or more left usable loops for each theme searched (Japanese, fantasy forest, Viking, epic choir, villain). Level them by sampling a long track in windows (below): four 2.6 s windows per track gave mean RMS from 0.080 to 0.201, and volumes from 0.8 to 0.32 evened them.
- Choose by description, then measure (below) before it goes in the sound table.
- **Audio from a reference video** *(default)*: extract it (`ffmpeg -i REF.mp4 -vn -ss A -to B -c:a libmp3lame -q:a 2 OUT.mp3`) only once the user says that clip's audio is cleared for use, and only from the clip they name. A clearance never carries to another clip: ask which when none is named, and never pick one for them. If Roblox rejects or removes the upload, report it and keep the track it would have replaced.

## Measure
- **Probe silently:** wire an `AudioPlayer` only to an `AudioAnalyzer`, with no `AudioDeviceOutput` on the wire. Nothing reaches the speakers, but `RmsLevel` and `PeakLevel` read true levels, so many files can be compared at once, even in the owner's open session.
- **Bin a one-shot's loudness over time** (quarter-second bins) before trusting one peak reading. Bins show what a peak hides: a file holding a second and third hit, a long quiet tail, a candidate peaking at 0.04 that its name made sound usable.
- From the bins read duration, onset, peak time and loudness, and the spectrum (`AudioAnalyzer:GetSpectrum()`), so each sound lands on its beat and sits in the mix.
- **Warm each file first:** play it once to the end through the silent probe, then measure. A cold probe reads a file while it loads: three footstep files of 0.4 to 0.6 s and two other short effects played out before they had loaded and read `RmsLevel` 0 throughout, and longer tracks lost their first 0.5 to 1 s. After one warm play the same probe read every onset and loudest moment.
- **Subtract a load delay first:** exact zeros at the start of a reading are the file starting late, not silence in it. In one probe of 13 files every envelope had one nonzero bin, then 0.6 s of exact zeros, then the sound, so every onset and peak read 0.6 s late; another probe had no delay. Find the leading exact-zero bins and subtract them before setting Start or Hit times.
- **Probe in Edit too, and set each cue's volume from its peak:** the silent probe read all 122 PSE and APM candidates in Edit mode (a warm play, then 50 ms bins). Peaks ran from 0.03 to 1.0, so a volume per role mattered more than the pick *(default: about 0.35 for swings, 0.5 for hits and skills, 0.25 for UI)*. Read `TimeLength`, not the catalog's rounded duration (0.23 s against "0.3 s"), and prefer an onset within 0.1 s for hits and swings.
- **Sample a clip under 0.3 s every frame** (`task.wait()`), never in 50 ms bins (`task.wait(0.05)`), which under-read sounds of 0.25 s or less badly: a bubbly click read peak 0.12 (0.49 sampled every frame), hovers 0.00 (0.25-0.26), a 0.06 s tick 0.00 (0.51). Sampled every frame, all twelve gave usable peaks. A probe in Edit takes batches of 20 ids in parallel: wait for `IsReady`, a warm play, then a measured play logging `PeakLevel` and `RmsLevel`, with the leading zeros subtracted (above); 92 ids took about 6 calls.

## Play and mix
- Put ids in the project's sound table and play them through the project's audio path. Roblox now discourages `Sound`, `SoundGroup` and `SoundEffect` in favor of the Audio API ([audio objects](https://create.roblox.com/docs/audio/objects)): `AudioPlayer` -> `Wire` -> `AudioDeviceOutput` for 2D; `AudioPlayer` -> `Wire` -> `AudioEmitter`, heard through `AudioListener` -> `Wire` -> `AudioDeviceOutput`, for 3D; `AudioEqualizer`, `AudioCompressor` and `AudioReverb` on the wire. New audio systems use it; a project built on `Sound` stays consistent (`Volume`, `RollOffMaxDistance` and a `SoundGroup` matching the existing audio) until it migrates.
- **A few buses into a limiter** *(default)*: scene, clean (UI and stingers) and music, each wired into an `AudioLimiter` before the output. `AudioLimiter.MaxLevel` is decibels, from -12 to 0 (so -1, never 0.9).
- **Meter each bus during a real match**, with an `AudioAnalyzer` on it: layers that are fine alone stack (three sounds on one beat summed to 1.86 before the limiter; trimmed, 1.36). Trim or stagger what lands together.
- **Music needs no preload:** an `AudioPlayer` keeps a `TimePosition` set before it has loaded, so set it and call `Play`; tracks not yet loaded were ready in about 0.24 s and started from the set point. A fade-in covers that, and phones keep the memory. Music may play on an `AudioPlayer` (its `Asset`), not a `Sound`: look for both.
