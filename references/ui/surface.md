# UI: faded backgrounds and shine

- **Identify color shading versus an opacity fade before building.** A shade behind a stat or text row that disappears into the game view needs a [UIGradient](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/UIGradient) with a varying `Transparency` `NumberSequence`. A uniformly translucent rectangle has a hard edge and does not reproduce that fade; changing `Color` alone does not fade to transparency.
- Put the gradient on a separate background Frame behind the icon and text, so the content stays fully visible. Set the Frame's base `BackgroundTransparency` low enough for the shade to show, then use gradient transparency keypoints to fade from the intended opacity to `1` at the disappearing edge. Match the fade's direction and extent with `Rotation`, `Offset` and keypoint positions. Keep `Color` constant for a pure opacity fade. In Studio-only work, author these instances and properties directly; no runtime script is needed.
- **Shine stays inside the face, clear of its strokes.** Frames are acceptable for shine bands, but size and inset their visible area from the innermost edge of every border/rim, including its thickness, offset, rounded corners and device scale. Keep the border continuous and unobscured. Match the reference's clearance; otherwise keep the normal 2 px interior margin. Rotating or animating a band must not carry its corners across the rim.
- Use a separate inset face for gloss and textures, with the outline drawn above it. Verify the actual rendered mask: a parent's `UICorner` or rectangular `ClipsDescendants` alone is not proof that a rotated child is contained by the rounded face. Use inset geometry or a suitable masked image when needed. A higher border `ZIndex` alone does not fix shine that extends outside the face.
- Only let shine cross a stroke when that overlap is explicitly present in the reference or requested design. Record that specific exception; do not infer it merely because the effect is decorative.
